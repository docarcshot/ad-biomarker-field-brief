Core priority includes blood and CSF biomarkers; amyloid, tau, and relevant FDG PET; analytical validity and preanalytics; assay/platform and head-to-head comparisons; cutoffs, gray zones, predictive values, prevalence effects, and comorbidity effects; PET acquisition, visual reading, quantification, Centiloid/SUVR methods, tracer comparability, structured reporting, and reader reproducibility; blood-CSF-PET concordance and discordance; sequential/reflex testing workflows; treatment eligibility, response and safety monitoring; laboratory and imaging implementation; authorization, availability, coverage, and diagnostic guidance. Search explicitly for these concepts rather than relying on generic Alzheimer terms. High-value systematic reviews, technical reviews, practice parameters, consensus-to-practice papers, and negative studies qualify when they materially change interpretation or implementation; original cohort data are not required.

General Alzheimer clinical trials, guidelines, safety, symptomatic care, nonpharmacologic care, caregiver interventions, risk reduction, and access remain in scope, but are secondary context. A non-core item qualifies only if it is practice-changing, changes a formal recommendation or access pathway, reports a pivotal or clearly decision-relevant trial, or resolves a major field question. Exclude routine association studies, small feasibility pilots, generic screening-model papers, lifestyle correlations, and low-impact caregiver or management studies unless the evidence is unusually strong and actionable.
